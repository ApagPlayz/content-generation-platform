# Voiceover quality — research

## What I did
- Looked into why the true-crime documentary voice sounds robotic.
- **Found the cause:** the app is using your Mac's built-in voice as a free fallback, and only the *old, low-quality* voices are installed on this machine (Samantha, Albert, etc.). Those are the robotic-sounding ones. The good voice service the app already supports (ElevenLabs) isn't switched on yet.
- Researched the best-sounding options available in 2026, from "free and instant" to "studio quality."

## What I recommend next
Three options, cheapest to best:

1. **Free, 5-minute fix — download better Mac voices.**
   Go to System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → System Voice → "Manage Voices," and download a **Premium** or **Enhanced** voice (e.g. *Ava (Premium)*, *Tom (Enhanced)*). Much more natural, still $0, still fully on your computer. Good enough for testing; still not quite documentary-grade.

2. **Free, best local quality — add a modern AI voice that runs on your Mac (recommended).**
   A model called **Kokoro** sounds clear and professional, is free to use commercially, and runs on your machine with no monthly cost. This fits the "local, $0" goal best. (If you ever want it to *clone a specific narrator's voice*, a model called Chatterbox does that, but it needs more horsepower.)

3. **Paid, easiest top-tier — turn on ElevenLabs (or Fish Audio).**
   The app is already wired for ElevenLabs; it just needs an account key added. This gives the most emotional, human documentary delivery with zero setup work. Costs roughly 18 cents per ~1,000 characters of script.

**My suggestion:** do #1 right now so the next test sounds dramatically better, and let me build in #2 (Kokoro) as the new default free voice so every video sounds natural without any ongoing cost.
