## What I did
- **Dug into the three things you flagged** — that the videos are just zooming photos with no
  real video, no building tension (rising action / climax), and no real "hook" to grab people.
  I ran deep research on all three and wrote a full plan. Here's the short version.
- **Real video footage (fixes the "just zooming" problem).** There are free, legal libraries
  of actual moving clips we can pull automatically — rain on a window, an empty courtroom,
  night driving, police lights, etc. The best one (Pexels) is free, allows commercial use, and
  needs no credit. We'd match a clip to each part of the story. We deliberately use **generic
  mood footage only — never real footage of the actual people or crime**, which is also the
  legally safe choice (a true-crime YouTuber just lost a $17.5M lawsuit for getting this wrong).
- **Tension and story arc.** Right now the narration is one flat block. The fix is to write each
  video in **beats** — hook, setup, the detail that doesn't add up, rising complications, a
  twist, the climax/reveal, then a short ending. The climax should land near the end (about
  80% in), with a little "re-hook" in the middle so people don't drop off. Each beat connects
  with "but…" or "therefore…" (cause and effect), which is what actually builds suspense.
- **Hooks.** The first 2–3 seconds decide whether someone keeps watching. The plan generates
  ~10 different hook ideas per video, scores them, throws out anything that over-promises or
  isn't legally safe, and keeps the best one — shown as a punchy line of on-screen text plus a
  calm spoken line.
- **Wrote it all up** as a step-by-step build plan (saved in the project's docs), broken into
  phases so we can ship improvements one at a time.
