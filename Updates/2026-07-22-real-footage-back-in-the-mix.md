# Real footage is back — now mixed with the photos

## What I did
- Brought **real, on-topic film footage back into the videos** — but only footage
  that actually matches the story, never the old generic mood clips (no more
  tropical rain in a banking video).
- Videos now **mix moving footage AND multiple good photos**: photos stay the
  backbone (Wikipedia/Commons, which works well), and short relevant clips are
  laid over a few beats. Each clip is kept short (max 8 seconds on screen),
  silenced (your narration + music are the only sound), and credited in the
  video description.
- Clips come from two places: **archive.org** public-domain newsreels/film and
  **YouTube** news/press/documentary excerpts. A strict relevance check only
  lets through footage whose title actually names the story — so a Titanic video
  gets Titanic footage, not a random old reel.
- If a topic genuinely has no relevant footage, the video simply renders as a
  clean photo slideshow (that's fine and expected for some topics).
- **Proved it end-to-end**: I let the History factory make a real video. It
  picked "The Sinking of the Titanic", pulled **3 real Titanic film clips + 6
  photos**, and rendered a 76-second video. I pulled still frames out of the
  finished file and looked at them: paintings/photos of the sinking and the
  shipyard, plus a real moving clip of a ship interior during the "into
  lifeboats" line. All on-topic.
- Both factories (True Crime + History) are switched on for this, live.

## What I recommend next
- **Watch a couple of finished videos end-to-end** to sanity-check the pacing and
  that the clips feel relevant to you (I verified frames, not the full playback).
- The YouTube clip path is built and on, but on the Titanic run archive.org
  filled all 3 clip slots first, so YouTube wasn't needed. Worth watching a run
  on a more modern topic (where archive film is thinner) to see YouTube kick in.
- Optional: if you ever want *more* moving footage per video, I can raise the
  per-video clip cap (currently 3 beats) — but photos-as-backbone is the safer
  default and what you asked for.
